Design of a Synchronous Reluctance Machine

An analytical study of synchronous reluctance machine design, magnetic saliency, stator design and TLA flux-barrier rotor optimization.

Overview

This individual report studies the design methodology of a synchronous reluctance motor (SynRM), from general rotating-machine dimensions and winding considerations to rotor anisotropy, torque production and optimization of flux barriers.

The study also compares the SynRM with an induction motor and discusses why the absence of normal steady-state rotor currents can reduce rotor electrical losses.

Electrical-machine design process

The report frames machine design as an iterative sequence: starting values, main dimensions, air gap, winding selection, flux density, number of turns, tooth and slot dimensions, magnetic voltage, saturation checks, yoke sizing and finally calculation of machine characteristics.

Reluctance torque and magnetic saliency

In the d-q frame, the d-axis is designed as the lower-reluctance magnetic path and the q-axis as the higher-reluctance path. The resulting inductance difference is fundamental to torque production.

Te = 3/2 · p · (Ld − Lq) · idiq
Saliency ratio ξ = Ld / Lq

A larger useful separation between the direct- and quadrature-axis inductances increases the machine's reluctance-torque capability.

Simplified loss comparison

The report derives a simplified copper-loss comparison in which the SynRM/induction-motor copper-loss ratio becomes 0.5 under specific assumptions about currents and resistances. This is treated here as an illustrative derivation, not a universal 50% efficiency claim.

TLA rotor and flux-barrier geometry

The report focuses on a transversely laminated anisotropic (TLA) rotor because it is more compatible with conventional laminated-machine manufacturing than an axially laminated anisotropic rotor.

To make the geometry tractable, the technical study considers a single flux barrier and examines q-axis and d-axis insulation ratios, barrier width, barrier angle and radial position.

FEM-based optimization framework

Analytical geometry gives an initial design, but the report notes that leakage flux and rotor-core saturation require finite-element analysis for refinement. The optimization objectives are framed around improving saliency and torque while reducing torque ripple.

  • Air-gap thickness
  • Flux density in rotor segments
  • Barrier-to-segment thickness ratio
  • Barrier shape and placement
  • Number of barriers
  • d- and q-axis insulation ratios

What this project does — and does not — claim

The report is an individually authored design study covering analytical and FEM-based approaches. It does not document a personally created FEM mesh, boundary-condition setup or original numerical field map.

Portfolio wording. The supported claim is: “Studied analytical and FEM-based approaches for synchronous reluctance motor rotor design and optimization.”

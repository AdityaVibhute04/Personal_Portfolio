Wind Turbine Design for Highway Energy Harvesting

Literature-driven aerodynamic design, fabrication and experimental assessment of a Savonius vertical-axis wind turbine concept for harvesting airflow near highways.

Overview

The project explored whether airflow associated with highway environments could be harvested using a compact Savonius vertical-axis wind turbine. The broader application considered highway lighting, while the course project focused on designing and demonstrating a scale turbine-generator prototype.

A Savonius rotor was chosen for its omnidirectional operation, modest height requirement and suitability for turbulent flow conditions.

Portfolio framing. The full-scale concept and the laboratory proof-of-concept are kept separate here because the report references different lighting-power targets in different sections.

Aerodynamic design

Rather than running computationally expensive CFD for many geometries, the team used published experimental studies to select the rotor parameters. At an average design velocity of 5 m/s and target tip-speed ratio of about 0.8, the rotor diameter was estimated at 0.46 m.

Prototype fabrication

The model was fabricated using readily available materials including cardboard, Depron, a steel shaft, bearings, a small DC generator, wiring and an LED load. Structural supports were added after rotor oscillation became a practical issue.

Experimental assessment

Airflow was produced with a hair dryer at three operating conditions. Rotor speed was estimated from video frame analysis, while generator voltage and current were measured with a multimeter.

The highest reported electrical output was approximately 0.5 W. Generator voltage remained below the level required to directly light the intended LED in the test configuration.

Why the prototype underperformed

  • Cardboard blades did not reproduce the intended geometry precisely.
  • Low structural rigidity introduced deformation and rotor wobble.
  • Bearings improved alignment but introduced additional rotational resistance.
  • The model did not reach the operating conditions assumed during the analytical design.

The report identifies more rigid fabrication materials, such as PVC, as one route to improving the prototype.

Self-Sustaining Wireless Gas Pipeline Monitoring

A laboratory proof-of-concept combining in-pipe energy harvesting, pressure sensing, embedded control and LTE-based remote monitoring.

Overview

The project investigated a locally powered monitoring node for underground gas pipelines. A small horizontal-axis turbine extracts energy from the flow, a generator converts the mechanical power to electricity, and the electrical system supports pressure sensing and wireless transmission.

The lab prototype used an Arduino UNO, BME280 pressure sensor, 4G LTE module, DC motor used as a generator, and a buck converter.

Scope. This page presents the work as a laboratory proof-of-concept. It does not claim that the prototype is ready for deployment in an operating natural-gas pipeline.

System architecture

The system joins energy conversion and sensing into a single chain:

Pipeline flow → Turbine → Generator → DC-DC conversion → Sensor + Arduino → LTE → Phone

Energy-harvesting concept

The design calculations considered a 5 cm diameter HAWT in 20 m/s airflow. With an assumed coefficient of power of 0.48, the report estimated 9.45 W in the wind and approximately 4.54 W extractable by the turbine.

Hardware iteration and pipe testing

The team compared several blade and motor combinations before integrating the generator into the pipe setup. Open-flow testing reported approximately 20 m/s near the turbine and around 28 V generator output. After enclosing the turbine, the report records approximately 13 m/s at the far end of the pipe and around 24 V generator output.

Voltage wording. Different sections of the source report mention roughly 24 V, 24.2 V and 28 V. This page labels them by test context instead of presenting a single universal generator voltage.

Simulated leak detection

An adjustable valve was used to simulate a leak. Opening the valve produced a pressure drop, while the pressure remained comparatively stable when the valve was closed. Measurements were transmitted remotely through the LTE communication chain.

The demonstrated outcome is best described as remote pressure monitoring with simulated leak events — not a machine-learning leak classifier.

From prototype to field deployment

The report discusses how a real installation would need substantially more robust design choices: industrial sensing, hazardous-area electrical design, sealing, corrosion resistance, protected enclosures, pressure-relief provisions and emergency shutdown systems.

It also suggests more durable turbine materials, industrial-grade controllers, improved generators and weather-resistant connections for long-term operation.
